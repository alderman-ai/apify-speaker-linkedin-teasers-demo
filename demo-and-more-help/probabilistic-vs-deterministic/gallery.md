# Gallery: the same code, different words

Every image below came out of the same pipeline. Open two or three of them
side by side — ideally one folklore character next to one real person — and
look for the difference.

You will not find one in the layout. The card sits in exactly the same place
at exactly the same size on every image. The starfield behind it, the orange
frame and accents, the type sizes, the spacing, the little
help icon in the footer, the "Join me in PRAGUE" line: identical, down to the
pixel, because a template and a fixed set of measurements put them there.
None of that is decided fresh each time, so none of it can drift.

What does change is the part that should: the speaker's name, their role, the
company, the topic, the length, the one-line blurb, the portrait and the
logo. Those are words and pictures, and words are where a language model
genuinely helps — it can write a dozen different blurbs that each sound like a
person. It can also write a blurb that is forty characters too long, and that
is the risk. So the pipeline holds every text field to a character budget and
stops rather than shrink the words or stretch the card.

That is the whole argument of this folder in one look. Creative variety where
variety is the point, and none at all where your brand lives. A marketing team
gets a hundred of these without a hundred rounds of checking that the logo is
still the right size.

Two things worth knowing before you reuse anything here. Some of the fictional
portraits and one logo come from CC BY-SA sources, so publishing them carries
an attribution obligation — the credit lines sit in the folders. And the
real-people set uses real names and likenesses for an internal test only, so
please don't publish any of it as real event material. And the card design
itself is an unofficial, non-commercial homage: Apify's name, logo and visual
design belong to Apify, so these are community-event teasers, not Apify
material.
